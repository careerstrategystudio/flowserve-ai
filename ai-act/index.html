Is your business AI Act ready?

If you use a chatbot, automatic WhatsApp replies, AI-written posts or an AI phone line, new EU rules already apply to you. Here are the eight things an Irish salon, clinic or small business should have in place.

  1. Tell people when they are talking to AI

    If a chatbot, WhatsApp assistant or AI phone line talks to your clients, it must say so clearly at the start — not in the small print. This applies from 2 August 2026 (Article 50 of the EU AI Act).

  2. Always offer a human

    Clients should be able to reach a real person easily — for complaints, refunds, anything sensitive or anything the AI doesn’t understand.

  3. Label AI-generated images and video

    If you post AI-generated or AI-edited images, audio or video that look real (for example a ‘before and after’), disclose that they were generated or manipulated with AI.

  4. Train the people who use AI

    Since February 2025 the AI Act asks businesses using AI to make sure their staff understand it well enough to use it safely (Article 4). The EU has proposed softening this for everyday tools, so check the current status — but a short written briefing for your team is good practice either way.

  5. Know where your clients’ data goes

    Every AI tool that sees client names, phone numbers or messages is processing personal data under GDPR. You need a written data processing agreement with each provider and to know if data leaves the EU.

  6. Keep health information out of chatbots

    Allergies, medication, skin conditions and pregnancy are special-category data under GDPR. Don’t let an AI assistant collect or answer questions about them — hand those to a qualified person.

  7. Don’t let AI make up answers

    Prices, opening hours and cancellation rules should come from information you approved, not from what the AI ‘thinks’. Businesses have been held responsible for what their chatbot promised.

  8. Keep a record

    Keep a log of what your AI tools sent and why, and a one-page note of which tools you use and for what. If a client complains, you can show what happened.

This checklist is general information, not legal advice. Rules and guidance are still evolving — last reviewed 7 October 2026. For your specific situation, speak to a solicitor or the Data Protection Commission’s guidance.
